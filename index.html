What do I do right now?

🌿 He bolts toward the next smell

Prevent speed buildup where possible: use a manageable leash radius and position yourself before he accelerates. If tension starts, stop safely; don't let pulling deliver him to the smell. Invite a turn back, reward slack, then approach together for 1–3 easy loose-leash steps and release with “Smells!” Practice at easier scents if this repeats.

👃 He is locked onto one smell

If safe, allow some sniffing. To leave, use a cheerful attention sound first; once he orients toward you, say “Let's go” and reward movement. If he cannot disengage, avoid repeating the cue; gently manage away and make the next practice easier. Occasionally let him return after leaving.

🐕 An excited dog is across the street

While Dooley can still respond, reward noticing the dog and continuing with you. Keep moving if safe. If he fixates, increase distance when possible. If no escape route exists, use secure, brief leash management to pass; don't drag him or demand a sit. Reward reengagement after passing.

🚲 A bike appears suddenly

Prioritize safety: keep him secure, step aside if safe, and let the bike pass. Don't attempt difficult training during a surprise encounter. Practice bikes later at a distance where he can take treats and remain responsive.

🐇 A rabbit runs

Secure the leash; don't approach wildlife. If Dooley notices and keeps moving without pulling, mark and reward. If he fixates, create distance and continue. A history of not lunging is encouraging, not a guarantee.

😣 I'm getting frustrated

Stop the training exercise. Move to an easier area, use safe leash management, and finish the walk. A difficult outing isn't a failed dog.
